## 1. Executive Summary & Philosophy: Design Before Code

This document represents the **authoritative, non-code architectural and civil engineering specification** for the Sri Harish Rajoori multi-generational family residence on Property 2 in Karimnagar, Telangana.

In professional civil architecture, software code, CAD coordinate scripts, and 3D visualizers are merely downstream tools used to execute an approved design. When automated systems jump straight into coordinate scripting without a rock-solid, common-sense architectural plan, common sense fails: doors open into furniture, walking from stairs to the front door leaves residents drenched in monsoon downpours, utility rooms trap kitchen cooks, and bathrooms open awkwardly toward dinner guests.

This document serves as the **human-readable single source of truth**. Every room dimension, wall junction, structural column, door swing, window sill, and circulation pathway detailed herein has been designed with rigorous civil engineering standards, municipal bye-laws (TG-bPASS / Karimnagar Municipal Corporation), and strict Telangana Vaastu Shastra principles.

## 2. The 10 "Common-Sense & Civil Engineering Failures" and Their Solutions

Before examining room dimensions, here is how this specification methodically resolves every common-sense flaw present in typical architectural drafts:

| # | Common-Sense / Civil Flaw | Why It Fails in Real Life | The Engineered Solution in This Plan |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Exposed External Core Walkway** | Placing stairs/lift on the West while placing the main door at North-North-East forces residents to walk across an exposed ledge in Karimnagar’s 44°C summer heat or torrential monsoon rains. | **Continuous Covered 4'-3" North Promenade**: The upper-floor arrival landing connects directly into a deep, covered cantilever verandah promenade sheltered by a 5'-0" overhead roof slab, giving complete rain and sun protection from lift to front door. |
| **2** | **Utility Balcony Through Kitchen** | Forcing residents or domestic help to walk through the kitchen cooking triangle past hot stoves and boiling oil to reach the washing machine and utility sink. | **Independent Dining-Lobby Access**: The out-of-house utility balcony is located on the East exterior and entered via a dedicated door from the dining circulation lobby, completely bypassing the kitchen. |
| **3** | **Exposed Column in Living Hall** | A structural column protruding 9" into the middle of the living room or circulation aisle creates a permanent collision hazard and ruins aesthetics. | **100% Flush Column Encasement**: Structural Column C11 is fully embedded within the continuous 9" masonry dividing wall separating the Living Hall from the Pooja/Mallanna enclave. Zero exposed columns in any room. |
| **4** | **Common Bath Facing Living Room** | Guests or family members exiting the common bathroom step directly into full view of people seated on the living room sofa or at the dining table. | **Private Residential Lobby with Shielded Door**: Bathrooms are clustered on the West exterior wall inside a 5'-7" x 13'-4" private lobby. The door is oriented West, completely invisible from the living room sofa. |
| **5** | **Inter-Bedroom Traffic** | Having to enter one bedroom to reach another room, an office, or a bathroom destroys acoustic and personal privacy. | **Zero Inter-Bedroom Circulation**: Master Bedroom, NW Bedroom/Office, and Common Bath each open independently into the Private Lobby. Bedroom 2/3 opens directly to the Living Hall. |
| **6** | **Misoriented / Oversized Pooja** | Building an oversized 12' room for daily prayers, entering from the South, or placing deities facing into walls or facing South. | **Compact 4'-0" Max Daily Pooja**: Sized to 4'-0" width x 7'-6" length. Door on West wall. Deity faces East. Mallanna Room sized to 8'-4" x 8'-0" with altar on South wall facing North. |
| **7** | **Pooja Touching Kitchen Heat** | Sharing a wall between the holy temple altar and the hot kitchen cooking zone, exhaust duct, or plumbing sink. | **2'-0" Detached Acoustic & Thermal Cavity**: The Mallanna Temple Room is physically detached from the SE Kitchen by a double-wall buffer cavity. |
| **8** | **Blocked / Dark Ishanya (NE)** | Filling the sacred North-East corner with heavy walls, stairs, or overhead water tanks blocks the morning UV light and spiritual energy. | **Open Ishanya Sitout & NNE Double Entrance**: NE corner features an open sitout with glazed French doors adjacent to the Simhadwaram, flooding the entire living hall with morning sunlight and cool breezes. |
| **9** | **Misaligned Plumbing Stacks** | Placing bathrooms in different locations on different floors causes horizontal sewage pipe runs in false ceilings that clog, leak, and crack. | **True Vertical Plumbing Stacking**: Attached and common bathrooms, kitchens, and utility sinks stack identically across all floors, dropping straight into a single external plumbing duct on the West/East walls. |
| **10**| **Inaccessible Building Perimeter** | Inability to clean external windows, service AC outdoor units, or inspect walls without hiring scaffolding. | **Continuous 360° Cantilever Walk-Around Slab Gallery**: 4'-3" North, 3'-6" East, 3'-6" South, and 3'-0" West slabs wrap the entire house. |

## 4. Structural RCC Framing System & Column Grid

The building is an RCC framed structure adhering to **IS 456:2000** (Plain and Reinforced Concrete) and **IS 1893:2016** (Earthquake Resistant Design for Zone II).

### 4.1 Structural Column Grid ($4 \times 4 = 16$ Columns)
All columns are standard **9" x 18" (230 mm x 450 mm)** M25-grade RCC columns.
- **Grid Lines East-West (X-Coordinates from West Plinth Edge)**:
  - **Grid X1**: $X = 0.00\text{ m}$ ($0'-0"$ - West Plinth & External Wall Line)

### 4.2 Absolute Column Encasement Rule
- **Column C11 at $(X = 7.32\text{ m}, Y = 8.13\text{ m})$**: In preliminary coordinate drafts, this column appeared standing open inside the living space. In this plan, the continuous 9" dividing wall separating the Living Hall from the Daily Pooja Mandir runs directly through C11, embedding it 100% within flush plasterwork.
- **Result**: Zero exposed structural columns in any living, dining, bedroom, or circulation space.

### 6.1 The Engineering Solution for Protected Movement
- **The Problem**: In Karimnagar, monsoon storms feature heavy driving rain and summer temperatures exceed 44°C. Walking along an uncovered 3-foot exterior ledge to reach the front door is unacceptable.
- **The Solution**:
  1. The Upper Floor Arrival Landing connects directly to a **4'-3" wide continuous cantilever promenade deck** along the North facade.
  2. The RCC roof slab projects **5'-0"** overhead, providing full rain shading and sun deflection.
  3. A 3'-6" high stainless steel (SS 304) and laminated toughened glass safety railing lines the promenade.
  4. Walking from the lift or stairs to the Simhadwaram is a comfortable, sheltered 18-foot promenade walk overlooking the 17-foot front garden lawn.
